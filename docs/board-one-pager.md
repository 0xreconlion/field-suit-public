# Using AI for our reporting — what the board is being asked to adopt

*The `nonprofit` profile's governance artifact. An owner-operated business needs
the equivalent written for its owner, not this handed over unchanged — see
`65-vertical-profiles.md`.*

*Plain-language summary for board adoption. One page. No technical detail is
withheld here; there simply isn't any that changes the decision.*

---

## What is being proposed

A tool that helps staff **draft** our regular impact report by pulling figures
from the systems we already use, and showing where each figure came from.

It drafts. A person reviews and signs. That does not change.

## What it is allowed to touch

Only **summary totals** — the same aggregate numbers we already publish or
already report to funders.

It will **not** have access to individual client or participant records. Not
restricted, not discouraged — it has no permission to reach them and no way
to ask for one. Any work that would ever involve individual records would
come back to this board as a separate decision.

## What it is not allowed to do

- It cannot change anything in any of our systems. It can only read.
- It cannot send anything to anyone.
- It cannot make any decision about a person — eligibility, services,
  employment, or anything affecting a young person. Those stay with staff
  permanently, regardless of how well the tool performs.
- It cannot use a number it cannot trace back to one of our own documents.

## What happens when something is wrong

This is the part worth reading closely, because it is where most of the value
is.

- **If two of our systems disagree about a number**, it stops and tells us.
  It does not choose one. We expect this to happen, and finding out where our
  numbers disagree is one of the main reasons to do this.
- **If a source is out of date**, the report says so *next to the number* —
  not in a footnote at the end.
- **If a document we need is missing**, it stops. It does not produce a
  partial report, because a report that quietly left something out looks
  finished.
- **If it cannot show where a number came from**, it refuses to use it.

## Cost

There is a spending limit set per report. If a run would go over it, it stops
before spending the money rather than after. We will see what each report
costs.

## Who is accountable

| | |
|---|---|
| Sponsor | *(name)* |
| Can stop this at any time, for any reason | *(name)* |
| Day-to-day operator | *(name)* |

The third row is the one that matters in twelve months. If that person leaves
and nobody replaces them, this should be switched off rather than left
running unattended.

## What we are honestly not certain about

- It checks that a number matches the document it came from. It **cannot tell
  us whether that document is correct.** Our underlying record-keeping is
  still our responsibility.
- Whether this saves staff time is something we will **measure**, not assume.
  The measurement includes the time spent checking the output, because a tool
  that writes faster and takes longer to verify has saved nothing.
- If our funding agreements place conditions on how participant data is
  handled, those need to be read by someone qualified. We are not relying on
  the tool's judgement about that, or on the consultant's.

## What the board is adopting

1. A written policy that this tool may read **summary figures only**.
2. The list of decisions that stay with people permanently.
3. A named person who can stop it.
4. A review at *(interval)* covering what it produced, what staff had to
   correct, and whether it is still worth running.

Point 4 is a real gate. If corrections are not getting less frequent over
time, that is the signal to stop — and we will have the record to see it.
